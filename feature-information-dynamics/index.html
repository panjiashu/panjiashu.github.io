TL;DR

What does diffusion generate at each noise level? We answer this quantitatively through feature information dynamics, using optimal denoising losses to trace when features emerge and in what order. This order changes across representations: only RAE exhibits an abstract-to-concrete hierarchy in our comparison, and diffusion models trained in RAE space converge fastest. Finding the optimal generation order and representation for each modality is an interesting future direction.

An autoregressive view of diffusion

Autoregression and diffusion make generation tractable through smaller prediction tasks: one predicts successive sequence elements, the other progressively removes noise. What implicit generation order does diffusion follow?

In Sander Dieleman’s blog, pixel-space diffusion is viewed as approximate spectral autoregression: low frequencies emerge before high frequencies. This account relies on the approximately power-law spectrum of natural images: low frequencies carry more signal power, so under white Gaussian noise they have higher effective SNR and emerge earlier during denoising. Learned latent representations need not preserve this spectral structure, so the same argument does not directly apply. Their markedly different convergence speeds make understanding what happens in each representation essential.

Across arbitrary representations, which features are generated at each SNR? We quantify how information about a feature—class, mask, or Canny edges—is distributed along diffusion, then study the order in which multiple features emerge.

Feature information density

Let \(X_\gamma\coloneqq\sqrt{\gamma}X+N\) be the noisy observation at SNR \(\gamma\), with independent standard Gaussian noise. Mutual information \(I(Y;X_\gamma)\) measures how much it contains about feature \(Y\). We define feature information density as its rate of change along log-SNR; I-MMSE connects this rate to optimal denoising losses:

\[\begin{aligned}D_Y^{(\ln\gamma)}&\coloneqq\frac{d}{d\ln\gamma}I(Y;X_\gamma)\\&=\frac{\gamma}{2}\bigl[m_{\varnothing}(\gamma)-m_Y(\gamma)\bigr].\end{aligned}\]

Here \(m_S(\gamma)\) is the minimum x-prediction loss (MMSE) given condition \(S\). Below, \(Y\) is the MNIST digit class. The unconditional denoiser predicts the clean image from the noisy image alone; the class-conditional denoiser also receives its class label. Their optimal losses are \(m_{\varnothing}\) and \(m_Y\), respectively.

The interactive example below traces how class information emerges during diffusion. The digit’s class becomes visually clear around \(\log_{10}\gamma \approx 1\), where accumulated class information also saturates. Feature information density further reveals that most class information emerges within \(\log_{10}\gamma \in [-2,0]\).

Chained information decomposition

Features can share information. Our chained information decomposition measures what each feature adds beyond those already given. The conditional mutual information \(I(X_\gamma;Y_k\mid Y_{\lt k})\) measures how much the noisy observation reveals about the next feature once the preceding features are known. We define its rate of change along log-SNR as the chained feature information density:

\[\begin{aligned}D_{k\mid k-1}^{(\ln\gamma)}&\coloneqq\frac{d}{d\ln\gamma}I(X_\gamma;Y_k\mid Y_{\lt k})\\[4pt]&=\frac{\gamma}{2}\bigl(m_{k-1}-m_k\bigr).\end{aligned}\]

Here \(m_k\) is the optimal denoising loss given the first \(k\) features; \(m_0\) is unconditional.

We apply this decomposition to frequency bands, ordered from low to high. Chaining makes spectral autoregression visible quantitatively. Independently measured frequency bands overlap; after conditioning on lower-frequency bands, their incremental information peaks in low-to-high frequency order. This quantifies when each band is generated along SNR.

Representations change generation order

When drawing an image, people naturally first decide what to draw, then sketch its overall shape, and finally add local details. This motivates examining features at different levels of abstraction: class, mask, and Canny. Does diffusion generate them in an abstract-to-concrete order—and does that order change across representations?

Applying chained information decomposition to class → mask → Canny and training the corresponding denoisers, we estimate their chained feature information densities, shown below for each representation.

§5.2.2 · Representation diagnostics

Does each modality have an optimal generation order?

AO-GPT finds that left-to-right language modeling converges much faster than a fixed random order, attributing this advantage to language’s sequential structure.

Our observation is suggestive: diffusion in RAE space generates class, mask, and Canny in that order, and diffusion models trained in this space converge faster than those trained in pixel, SDVAE, and VAVAE spaces. We conjecture that images favor generation from abstract to concrete.

How can we discover the optimal generation order for each modality? Could those orders guide the next generation of generative models?
